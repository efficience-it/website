## Digital Technology, a Major Player in Carbon Emissions

The digital sector now holds a central place in our daily lives but is a major source of electricity consumption worldwide. Currently, the digital sector accounts for 3% to 4% of global greenhouse gas emissions and 2.5% of the national carbon footprint. Our [Green IT](/green-it) (in French) approach is part of this awareness.

## What If Our Digital Tools Were Real Enemies of the Climate?

According to a preliminary report from the French Senate on the environmental impact of digital technology, the sector's greenhouse gas emissions could rise significantly without intervention, with a projected increase of 60% by 2040, representing 6.7% of national GHG emissions.

To better understand how to reduce the ecological footprint of digital technology, it is essential to examine the sources of emissions. According to ADEME, emissions come from two main sources:

- **78%** generated by the manufacturing of electronic equipment, which mainly requires rare metals. Extraction involves highly polluting processes, often carried out in countries with high carbon footprints.
- **21%** coming from digital usage, particularly the data centers that support internet communications. These infrastructures consume substantial energy, mostly from fossil fuels worldwide, which are responsible for CO2 emissions.

## What Is Eco-Design?

Eco-design refers to an approach to designing products and services that aims to minimize environmental impact throughout their life cycle, from production to end of life. With growing ecological awareness and increasing legislative pressure, companies face growing incentives to adopt eco-design practices to reduce their ecological footprint, conserve resources and meet the expectations of consumers looking for sustainable solutions.

Digital eco-design seeks to limit the ecological impact of digital products and services throughout their life cycle, by considering various elements.

### Main Criteria Considered

- Material selection
- Reduced energy consumption
- Extended product lifespan
- Optimized transport and logistics
- Ease of recycling and end-of-life processing

Eco-design aims to reduce the ecological impact of products by making them more durable, resource-efficient and compatible with the principles of the circular economy. This approach involves every player in the product life cycle: companies, public services, consumers and users, each playing a role in the use and renewal of products.

Implementing this approach requires several essential criteria. Beyond best practices, regulations also frame eco-design, ensuring that products meet strict environmental impact standards.

## What Does the Regulation Say?

Of course, regulations govern eco-design related to energy efficiency. This regulation states that any product presented as eco-designed must give consumers accurate information justifying that claim:

- Qualitative and quantitative data on the environmental impact reductions achieved through eco-design processes
- Specification of the eco-designed elements (product, component, packaging, etc.) when eco-design only concerns portions of the marketed products, including packaging
- Eco-design definitions compliant with the ISO/TR14062 standards or directive 2009/125/EC
- Main environmental characteristics of product/packaging combinations

Data security is an absolute priority. Poorly secured applications expose companies to significant risks, including the loss of sensitive or confidential data. In addition, the CNIL can impose penalties for non-compliance with security standards. Comprehensive audits detect vulnerabilities and propose suitable solutions, guaranteeing maximum data protection.

## In Our Case, the Web: How Do You Assess the Ecological Impact of a Web Page?

The ecological impact of a web page depends on the energy and resources used to run it. Browsing web pages creates environmental footprints, because every piece of data accessed requires storage and access, consuming resources. For example, Patagonia optimized its site by reducing image sizes and using servers powered by renewable energy, significantly lowering its carbon footprint.

## 7 Tools to Measure Your Website's Ecological Footprint

Companies use tools that measure the ecological footprint of their websites, responding to environmental, economic and strategic concerns. These tools let companies and developers become aware of the environmental impact of sites and discover ways to reduce it.

**Website Carbon Calculator:** This free online tool analyzes the CO2 emissions generated by your site, considering page size and server energy consumption.

**Ecoindex:** This free Chrome extension offers an assessment through an environmental score from 0 to 100 based on DOM complexity, the weight of data transfers and the number of HTTP requests. Considering these criteria, the tool provides not only grades from A to G but also recommendations to improve energy efficiency.

**Ecograder:** Also free, Ecograder evaluates your site's environmental performance on several criteria: loading speed, code efficiency, renewable energy location.

**Beacon:** Beacon calculates the site's carbon footprint based on users' data consumption. It also offers recommendations to reduce energy impact through design and content optimization.

**Greenly:** This paid tool checks whether your site is hosted on servers powered by renewable energy, a key factor in reducing the ecological footprint.

**Green-IT Analysis:** This Chrome extension evaluates the site's environmental impact based on criteria such as the number of requests, page size, water used and GHGs produced. It offers optimization advice, including limiting domains and optimizing images.

## A Few Best Practices to Adopt

To integrate eco-design effectively into websites, some key practices must be adopted, particularly in hosting, design and development.

### Website Hosting

- Choose hosts committed to reducing energy consumption and to recycling. GreenGeeks is one example, using renewable energy and recycling electronic components to reduce ecological impact.
- Adjust service quality and availability levels to real needs; do not run several machines if you only need one. Our [Symfony hosting](/hebergement-symfony) (in French) offering builds in these principles of sobriety.
- Minify CSS and JavaScript files and enable caching through a CDN. [Caching strategies](/article/tout-savoir-sur-la-mise-en-cache-tips) (in French) also reduce server load and therefore energy consumption.

### Conception and Design

- Eliminate non-essential features
- Simplify user journeys to improve fluidity
- Adopt minimalist, lightweight designs suited to the web
- Reduce the amount of video and animation and/or optimize their quality

### Technical Development

- Reduce the number of HTTP requests to improve efficiency
- Use frameworks that standardize and optimize code. [Docker in production](/article/pourquoi-docker-est-indispensable-en-production-aujourdhui) (in French) also lets you size containers as precisely as possible
- Store static data locally and in cache to limit server calls
- Make the application installable on mobile with the techniques described in the article on [the web app manifest](/article/ameliorer-lexperience-utilisateur-grace-au-manifeste-des-applications-web) (in French), to offer a lightweight, high-performing experience
